Supplementary Material Index
============================

File: gmm_temporal_comparison.avi

Description
-----------
This video shows the full temporal evolution of the Gaussian Mixture Model (GMM)
reconstruction produced by the algorithm described in the manuscript. The animation
is laid out in three panels displayed side by side:

  Left panel  – Ground Truth: the true GMM at each time step, with each Gaussian
                component rendered as a filled ellipse (colour-coded by component
                index) and its ballistic centre-of-mass trajectory overlaid as a
                static path.

  Centre panel – Projections: the 1-D X-ray projection profiles at each time step,
                 showing both the observed data (ground truth) and the projections
                 synthesised from the current reconstruction, plotted against
                 receiver height.

  Right panel  – Reconstruction: the estimated GMM at each time step using the same
                 colour coding and trajectory overlay as the left panel, displayed in
                 the mirrored (receiver-side) coordinate frame to reflect the
                 measurement geometry.

The animation spans the full acquisition window and is rendered on an upsampled time
grid (8x the data sampling rate) to faithfully represent the continuous rotation of
each Gaussian component and avoid stroboscopic aliasing artefacts. Component
parameters include anisotropic shape matrices, attenuation coefficients, angular
velocities, and ballistic (position, velocity, acceleration) trajectories, all
recovered jointly by the four-stage optimisation pipeline.

Justification for Inclusion
----------------------------
Static figures in the manuscript can only show the reconstruction quality at a small
number of selected time instants. This video provides a complete, continuous view of
the reconstruction fidelity across the entire acquisition window, which is useful
for assessing the method's ability to track rapidly rotating and translating
Gaussian components.

The video is therefore a direct visual counterpart to the numerical results in the
manuscript and adds substantive evidential value beyond what can be conveyed in
printed figures.
